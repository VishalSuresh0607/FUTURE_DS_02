# FUTURE_DS_02 – Bank Customer Churn Prediction

## 📌 Project Overview

This project was completed as part of the **Future Interns Data Science & Analytics Internship – Task 2**.

## 📊 Key Findings

The dashboard provides an overview of customer characteristics and churn patterns across different demographic and account-related attributes.

### 👥 Customer Profile

- The dataset contains approximately **10K customers**.
- The customer base is relatively balanced by gender, with **54.57% male** and **45.43% female** customers.
- Approximately **51.51% of customers are active**, while **48.49% are inactive**.
- Around **70.55% of customers own a credit card**.

### 🌍 Geographic Distribution

- **France** represents approximately **50.14%** of the customers.
- **Spain** and **Germany** account for approximately **25.09%** and **24.77%**, respectively.
- This indicates that the dataset is primarily concentrated across these three countries.

### 💳 Product & Account Characteristics

- **Product 1** is the most commonly held product, accounting for approximately **50.84%** of customers.
- **Product 2** accounts for approximately **45.90%**.
- Products 3 and 4 represent only a small proportion of the customer base.
- Most customers fall within the lower and middle account-balance ranges, while customers with balances above **200K** represent a much smaller group.

### 📉 Churn Analysis

- The overall churn rate shown in the dashboard is approximately **20.4%**.
- Churn varies across different age groups, with the dashboard showing noticeably higher churn rates among some older customer groups.
- The **51–60 age group** shows a particularly high churn rate compared with most other age groups.
- Churn rates also vary across account-balance groups, with the highest displayed rate occurring in the **>200K balance** category.
- The dashboard indicates relatively similar churn-rate levels across most of the larger credit-score groups, while some very small customer groups show unusually high displayed rates.

### 🔎 Interpretation

These patterns can help identify customer segments that may require further investigation for retention strategies. However, the dashboard shows **associations within the dataset rather than proving that a particular demographic or account characteristic causes customer churn**.
